# Analytics findings

_Generated by `creaseiq analyze` from `reports/analytics.json`. Numbers are never typed by hand._

## 1. Winning the toss (causal test: the toss is random)
- Toss winners won **51.6%** of 1218 decided matches (95% Wilson CI 48.8%–54.4%; exact binomial p = 0.29).
- Effect vs a fair coin: +1.6 pp. With n = 1218 the minimum detectable effect (80% power) is ±4.0 pp, so the data **rule out toss advantages larger than about 4 pp** but cannot distinguish a small one from zero. This agrees with Sood & Willis (2016) for T20.
- The effect did not change measurably in the Impact Player era (difference -1.1 pp, 95% CI -7.7 to +5.5 pp, p = 0.74).

## 2. Batting second (chasing)
- The chasing side won **54.7%** (95% CI 51.9%–57.5%; p = 0.001). This is a real, modest advantage.
- Impact era minus earlier seasons: -2.5 pp (95% CI -9.1 to +4.1 pp, p = 0.46). There is no evidence that the chasing edge grew.
- *Associational:* toss winners who chose to field won 54.7% vs 45.3% for those who batted (χ² p = 0.002, Cramér's V = 0.09). The decision depends on conditions and team strength, so this gap is **not** a causal effect of choosing to field.

## 3. Home advantage
- Where exactly one side was at home (neutral seasons excluded), the home side won 53.2% of 900 matches (95% CI 50.0%–56.5%; p = 0.057). The effect is small and borderline.

## 4. The Impact Player era changed scoring
- Mean first-innings score rose from 162.6 to **189.7** (+27.1 runs, 95% CI 22.6–31.7; Welch p = 2.4e-27; Cohen's d = 0.82, a large effect).
- 200+ first innings: 10.1% before 2023 vs **41.7%** since.
- Implication for modelling: scoring features must be era-aware (an `impact_era` flag plus rolling windows).
